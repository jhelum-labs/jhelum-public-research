1. Executive Summary

Project Insight consolidates the evidence from four W-1.1 experiments: the Tiny Prototype, Project Tazi, Project Spark and Project Spectrum. It separates engineering success from language quality and uses only recorded measurements; unavailable values remain explicitly marked as not recorded.

Spectrum provides the strongest raw evidence because its JSONL log contains 80 unique evaluation steps after duplicate early records are resolved. Its final loss was 3.5749 training and 3.6465 validation. Its best validation loss was 3.6282 at step 19,000.

Across the programme, data quality and domain focus affected output quality more visibly than small differences in parameter count. Tazi learned broad English patterns from noisy web text but generated weak semantics. Spark achieved the best fluency inside its narrow story domain. Spectrum broadened domain coverage but produced dated encyclopedic style, repetition and factual-looking hallucinations.

Phase 5 verdict: The experimental programme successfully identified the pipeline, data, optimization and evaluation rules required before final-model design. Phase 6 may now begin, but the earlier 125M architecture document must be treated as a draft until its vocabulary, token budget and compute assumptions are re-evaluated.

5.1 Key Dataset Finding

Tazi and Spark were similar in model size, context and intended training length, yet their language quality differed sharply. Spark’s focused and simpler TinyStories distribution produced much lower validation loss and more coherent generation. This demonstrates that validation loss must be interpreted within each dataset rather than as a universal score across unrelated corpora.

Spectrum broadened the data distribution and consequently faced a harder prediction task. Its higher loss than Spark does not automatically mean that Spectrum is a worse general model; Spark’s very low loss is partly a consequence of its narrow, repetitive story domain.

7.1 Tazi Generation Finding

Tazi learned spelling, punctuation and sentence-like structure, but prompt relevance and long-range meaning remained weak. Repetition and invented words appeared, especially in technical prompts. The experiment was technically successful but not ready for instruction tuning or production use.

Figure 5. Exact Spectrum learning-rate schedule and recorded gradient norm across the cleaned run.

The learning rate warmed up and then decayed toward approximately 3×10⁻⁵. Gradient norms remained finite throughout the preserved records. The curve contains normal fluctuations but no NaN, infinity or catastrophic gradient explosion.

10.1 Generalization and Late-Stage Behavior

The best validation result occurred at step 19,000, while the final validation loss at step 20,000 was higher by 0.0183. This is a small late-stage regression, not severe overfitting. Best-checkpoint selection correctly preserved the stronger step-19,000 model.

12.1 Central Interpretation

The experiments show a progression from engineering validity to surface fluency, but none demonstrates reliable reasoning, factual accuracy or instruction following. The final model must therefore be evaluated as a base language model first; chat behavior should be added only after pretraining quality is established.

14. What Worked

  • A complete tokenizer → preprocessing → training → checkpoint → generation pipeline.
  • Memory-mapped uint16 token arrays for large processed datasets.
  • Warm-up, cosine learning-rate decay, AdamW, mixed precision and gradient clipping.
  • Best and latest checkpoints with interruption recovery.
  • Focused clean data for rapidly improving domain-specific fluency.
  • Fixed-prompt qualitative testing alongside numerical validation loss.
  • Preserving the tokenizer and configuration with each checkpoint.

15. What Did Not Work or Remains Weak

  • Judging capability from loss alone; low domain loss did not guarantee reasoning or broad knowledge.
  • Noisy broad data at small scale produced grammatical but semantically weak completions.
  • Missing explicit document boundaries and EOS stopping caused continuation into new documents or stories.
  • Long generations amplified repetition, topic drift and entity confusion.
  • Early experiment records did not always preserve complete raw logs, timing and GPU-memory metrics.
  • Generated names, dates and facts looked plausible but were unreliable.
  • The 256-token context restricted long-range learning and evaluation.

16. Requirements Derived for the Final Model

Area

Requirement established by Phase 5

Data

Quality filtering, deduplication, provenance, balanced domains and explicit EOS/document boundaries.

Tokenizer

Freeze once; archive vocabulary, merges, special IDs and hash with every checkpoint.

Architecture

Increase capacity and context, but choose dimensions only after compute and token-budget analysis.

Training

Warm-up + cosine decay, AdamW, clipping, mixed precision, best/latest checkpoints and exact resume.

Logging

Persist JSONL train/val loss, perplexity, LR, gradient norm, tokens/s, elapsed time and GPU memory.

Evaluation

Fixed held-out sets, per-domain losses, fixed prompts/seeds, repetition, EOS, factual and code tests.

Release

Preserve the base checkpoint before instruction tuning; document limitations and data categories.

17. Decisions for Phase 6 — Project Blueprint

Phase 6 should now plan and freeze the final W-1.1 model. It must not automatically inherit conflicting numbers from older planning documents. In particular, the attached architecture draft specifies 32,000 vocabulary tokens and a 5B-token target, while later project discussion considered 40,000 vocabulary tokens and 2B training tokens. Project Blueprint must resolve this conflict using tokenizer tests, compute estimates and data availability.

Decision to make

Evidence required before freezing

Vocabulary size

Tokenizer fertility for English, Urdu, Kashmiri, code, numbers and punctuation.

Training-token budget

Available clean data, GPU budget, time, cost and tokens-per-parameter trade-off.

Context length

Memory benchmark and long-document needs; compare 512, 1,024 and 2,048.

Exact architecture

Parameter-count unit test, memory estimate and small stability pilot.

Dataset mixture

Per-domain volume, quality, licensing and held-out evaluation plan.

Training platform

Confirmed BF16/FP16 support, storage throughput, checkpoint durability and total cost.

Freeze rule: No tokenizer, architecture, dataset mixture or optimizer constant should be silently changed after final pretraining begins. Any material change creates a new version.

18. Final Phase 5 Verdict

Research question

Answer

Does the W-1.1 pipeline work?

Yes — demonstrated from prototype through multi-domain training.

Can ~17M models learn fluent text?

Yes, especially with focused clean data.

Are the experimental models reliable assistants?

No — reasoning, factuality, relevance and long coherence remain insufficient.

Was overfitting the main problem?

No — capacity, data distribution and semantic learning were larger limits.

Is final-model planning justified now?

Yes — the main engineering and research lessons are documented.

Final Conclusion

W-1.1 Project Insight completes Phase 5. The four experiments established a functioning research pipeline and revealed the decisive roles of data quality, domain breadth, model capacity, context length, checkpoint discipline and honest evaluation. Spark delivered the strongest narrow-domain fluency; Spectrum provided the richest exact training evidence; Tazi proved broad-data stability; and the Tiny Prototype verified the original pipeline. None of the experiments should be presented as a factual or instruction-following assistant. Their combined value is the evidence they provide for a disciplined final-model design.

The programme may now proceed to Phase 6 — W-1.1 Project Blueprint, where the final architecture, tokenizer, context, data mixture, token budget, compute plan and evaluation gates will be selected and frozen.
