1. Executive Summary

W-1.1 Project Spectrum completed a 20,000-step pretraining experiment using a compact decoder-only Transformer and Spectrum’s own processed dataset and tokenizer. The reported loss began near 9.00 and finished at 3.69 for training and 3.71 for validation.

The final train–validation gap is only 0.02. This is a strong sign that the run did not exhibit severe overfitting at the final recorded point. However, the generated samples still show repetition, topic drift, dated source style and unreliable factual details. Loss convergence therefore did not equal assistant-quality generation.

Phase outcome: COMPLETE. Spectrum successfully produced readable English and supplied important evidence for Phase 5 and the later design of W-1.1-125M.

2. Experiment Objective

  • Train a broader-domain experimental model after the TinyStories-focused Spark phase.
  • Measure optimization stability over 20,000 updates on an NVIDIA T4 GPU.
  • Compare final training and validation behavior for signs of overfitting.
  • Test whether the model can generate readable completions across education, science, government and narrative prompts.
  • Extract practical lessons before planning the final W-1.1-125M model.

5. Perplexity and Generalization

Perplexity converts cross-entropy loss into a more intuitive scale using e^loss. Spectrum’s final estimated train perplexity is 40.04 and validation perplexity is 40.85. Lower is better.

Figure 3. Perplexity derived from the supplied final losses; values are rounded to two decimals.

The small validation gap suggests good final numerical generalization. It does not prove factual accuracy or long-range coherence. The generation samples show that those capabilities remain limited even when validation loss is close to training loss.

8. Overfitting Analysis

Final numerical evidence: no severe overfitting is visible from the supplied endpoint values. Validation loss is only 0.02 above training loss, approximately 0.54% relative to the training loss.

Limitation: a reliable overfitting diagnosis requires the complete train-loss and validation-loss history. If validation loss fell and then rose while training loss continued downward, that pattern could reveal late-stage overfitting even though the final gap is small. The JSONL training log should be preserved for the combined Phase 5 report.

9. What Spectrum Successfully Learned

  • English word formation, sentence boundaries and conventional punctuation.
  • Longer prose structure across several prompt types.
  • Dialogue formatting and formal expository style.
  • Names, dates, abbreviations and citation-like patterns present in the dataset.
  • A substantially better tokenizer–checkpoint match than the earlier corrupted local test.

10. Remaining Limitations

  • Topic drift after the first few sentences.
  • Phrase and concept repetition.
  • Weak semantic consistency over long generations.
  • Hallucinated biographical and historical details.
  • Strong imitation of dated source material.
  • No instruction tuning or reliable question-answering behavior.
  • Short 256-token training context limits long-range modeling.

11. Lessons for the Final W-1.1 Model

Observation

Design implication

Small final loss gap

Retain validation monitoring and best-checkpoint selection.

Readable but drifting prose

Increase model capacity and context length; improve document boundaries.

Dated/encyclopedic style

Rebalance and quality-filter the final dataset mixture.

Repetition

Improve data quality and evaluate decoding separately from training quality.

Unreliable facts

Treat the base model as a completion model; later add instruction tuning and retrieval where appropriate.

Tokenizer dependence

Archive tokenizer, configuration and checkpoint as one inseparable model package.

Final Conclusion

W-1.1 Project Spectrum completed its experimental purpose. Loss decreased from approximately 9.00 to 3.69 for training, while validation finished at 3.71. The 0.02 final gap indicates stable numerical generalization, and the model now generates readable English. The generation review also exposes clear limits in factuality, repetition, semantic continuity and topic control. These results should now be combined with Tiny Prototype, Tazi and Spark in the complete Phase 5 observation document before the final W-1.1-125M plan is designed.
