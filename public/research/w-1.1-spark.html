Final status: Phase 3 training completed successfully. The best SafeTensors checkpoint generates readable, structured short stories and is ready to be preserved as the Spark baseline for later W-1.1 experiments.

Executive Summary

W-1.1 Spark was trained as a controlled Phase 3 experiment to measure how a compact decoder-only Transformer learns from a large, focused story corpus. The final model completed its full 20,000-step cosine-decay schedule on a Tesla T4. Its best recorded validation loss was 1.4872, and fixed-prompt testing produced fluent multi-paragraph stories with recognizable characters, settings, dialogue, emotions and conclusions.

The experiment succeeded at language fluency and narrative form. It also exposed predictable limitations of a small, single-domain model: entity drift, weak causal reasoning, occasional grammar errors, repeated templates and unreliable document-ending behavior. These findings are useful inputs for Phase 4 Spectrum and the eventual W-1.1-125M design.

1. Phase 3 Purpose

Phase 3 was designed to improve the surface quality and stability of the W-1.1 experimental model before broadening its data. Spark focused on TinyStories because the corpus provides simple vocabulary, short narrative arcs and repeated grammatical patterns that a small model can learn efficiently.

2. Experiment Objectives

  • Train a compact language model on approximately 500 MB of focused story text.
  • Verify stable CUDA training on a Tesla T4 using FP16 mixed precision.
  • Measure training and validation behavior across a complete 20,000-step schedule.
  • Protect both the best checkpoint and the latest resumable checkpoint in Google Drive.
  • Evaluate grammar, story structure, repetition, semantic consistency and character tracking.
  • Create a reliable baseline for comparison with Phase 4 Spectrum.

6. Checkpoint and Resume Design

Spark stored checkpoints in Google Drive so a Colab interruption would not destroy training progress. The latest checkpoint restored model weights, optimizer state, completed step and best validation score. The best checkpoint was updated only when validation loss improved.

  • spark_best.safetensors - final model selected by lowest validation loss.
  • spark_latest.safetensors - weights from the most recent completed evaluation.
  • spark_latest_optimizer.pt - AdamW state required for an accurate resume.
  • spark_latest_state.json - completed step, configuration and best validation score.

8. Learning Behavior

Training remained stable under a peak learning rate of 3e-4, 500-step warm-up and cosine decay toward 3e-5. Earlier observations showed validation loss falling from approximately 4.1425 at step 250 to 1.6926 at step 5,750, then improving further to 1.5026 around step 15,500 and finally reaching a best recorded value of 1.4872 by completion.

Short increases in validation loss occurred during training, but later evaluations recovered and produced new best checkpoints. This behavior indicates evaluation noise and a late-stage plateau rather than a damaging loss explosion. Saving the best checkpoint protected the strongest model throughout the run.

Result: The selected learning-rate schedule was appropriate for Spark. It supported rapid early learning, stable middle-stage improvement and smaller late-stage refinements without NaN or infinite losses.

Four fixed prompts were used to test story fluency, structure, consistency and creativity. The model produced readable multi-paragraph stories in every case.

Representative Success: Tom's Mountain

The model introduced Tom's goal, followed his climb, described the view at the top and ended with a simple lesson about exploration. Character identity and the central setting remained stable through the story. This was the strongest evidence that Spark learned the basic beginning-middle-ending pattern of TinyStories.

Representative Limitation: Lily's Box

The prose remained fluent, but the mysterious box changed into hats, a trap and a cat-related incident. The response then introduced police without a convincing causal reason. This shows that Spark can imitate grammatical story form while still losing track of object identity and event logic.

13. Important Preprocessing Lesson

The preprocessing pipeline did not explicitly insert an <eos> token between every story, and the generation loop did not stop when an end token appeared. As a result, Spark sometimes completed one story and immediately began another. Future phases should define document boundaries explicitly during preprocessing and terminate generation when the end-of-sequence token is produced.

14. What Phase 3 Proved

Phase outcome: COMPLETE. A focused, clean corpus can teach a small model fluent grammar, paragraph structure and recognizable narrative patterns. However, fluency alone does not guarantee logical consistency, entity tracking, factual reliability or broad capability.

15. Lessons for W-1.1-125M

  • Preserve warm-up, cosine decay, gradient clipping and best-checkpoint selection.
  • Evaluate with fixed prompts and seeds in addition to validation loss.
  • Use explicit document separators and train reliable EOS behavior.
  • Track per-domain validation loss when the dataset becomes multi-domain.
  • Keep clean data and deduplication as first-class parts of the training system.
  • Do not confuse fluent text with reasoning; measure both separately.
  • Retain Spark as a baseline instead of overwriting it during later phases.

17. Next Step - Phase 4 Spectrum

The next controlled experiment is w-1.1-project-spectrum. It will test a balanced multi-domain dataset containing education, code and programming, web text, news and research. To isolate the effect of data diversity, the first Spectrum run should keep Spark's architecture, tokenizer size, context length, optimizer, learning-rate schedule and token budget as consistent as practical.

Final Conclusion

W-1.1 Spark successfully completed Phase 3. The approximately 17.34M-parameter model finished 20,000 CUDA training updates on a Tesla T4 and achieved a best recorded validation loss of 1.4872. Its best checkpoint produces readable, structured and often engaging short stories. The model remains limited in semantic consistency, entity tracking, causal reasoning and document termination, but those limitations are now measured rather than guessed. Spark should be frozen as the Phase 3 baseline and used to guide Spectrum and the final W-1.1-125M system.
