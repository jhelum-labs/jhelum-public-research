Executive summary

W-1.1 Project Instruct converted the pretrained W-1.1-125M causal language model into an instruction-following checkpoint using 300,000 question–answer and task examples. The run completed on an NVIDIA H100 80GB GPU in approximately 60 minutes and produced a final validation loss of 1.951 (perplexity 7.03).

1. Model and experiment definition

The project starts from W-1.1-125M, a pretrained next-token predictor. Instruction tuning does not replace next-token prediction: it teaches the same objective on structured prompts where the desired continuation is an answer. At inference time, the model still predicts one token at a time, conditioned on the instruction and all previously generated tokens.

Data-quality and contamination cautions

  • OpenOrca supplies 66.0% of the corpus, so its style and any duplicated or benchmark-derived content can dominate learned behavior.
  • GSM8K and SciQ were directly included in training; they should not be reported as clean independent benchmarks without a decontaminated holdout.
  • Public instruction datasets vary in factuality, formatting, licensing, safety coverage, and duplication. A production release needs documented filtering and provenance checks.

The run targeted 8,908 optimizer steps and completed 8,906. The two-step difference is operationally negligible and is consistent with sequence-count rounding or end-of-epoch batching. Checkpoints were committed throughout the run, enabling recovery.

Compute separation The notebook UI could remain on a small CPU kernel while the detached Modal function trained on H100. The notebook submitted and monitored the remote job; the GPU lived inside the training container.

Perplexity interpretation

Perplexity is exp(loss). For the final loss, exp(1.9508459) ≈ 7.03. This means the model’s average uncertainty on the held-out instruction tokens is equivalent to choosing among roughly seven plausible next tokens. It is a useful optimization signal, but it does not directly measure truthfulness, safety, or question-answer accuracy.

5. Qualitative generation tests

Post-training prompts showed clear paragraph-level fluency and instruction-style formatting. The model generated topical continuations for artificial intelligence, education, and narrative prompts. However, the samples also exposed hallmark small-model weaknesses: unsupported citations, repetition, semantic drift, and unreliable arithmetic.

The unweighted mean preferred score is 41.68%. This average is a convenient report-card summary, not an official aggregate benchmark because the tasks have different sizes, baselines, and difficulty.

What the scores say

  • Strongest relative result: PIQA at 58.98%, indicating some practical commonsense discrimination.
  • Moderate result: ARC Easy at 40.70%, showing partial elementary science knowledge.
  • Weakest areas: ARC Challenge, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, and OpenBookQA remain close to simple baselines.
  • BoolQ accuracy is below the dataset’s approximate majority baseline, so the raw percentage should not be interpreted as strong yes/no reasoning.
  • The full-run results are lower than several smoke estimates, demonstrating why small-sample scores should not be used for release claims.

Production definition

A model becomes usable by people only after the weights are wrapped in a reliable product system. For W-1.1 this means a versioned inference service, tokenizer/config packaging, prompt template, output controls, authentication, rate limits, logging, latency tests, abuse safeguards, evaluation gates, rollback, and clear user-facing limitations.

Recommended release label W-1.1 Instruct v1 — experimental research preview. Do not claim GPT-2 parity or general-answer accuracy until direct, reproducible comparisons and safety evaluations are complete.

Appendix C. Research conclusion

The W-1.1 instruction experiment is technically successful: a custom 123.7M-parameter pretrained model was converted into an answer-oriented checkpoint through a reproducible H100 workflow, and the final artifacts were preserved. The evidence does not support calling it a fully accurate or general production GPT. Its main value today is as a functioning research platform for data curation, fine-tuning, evaluation, quantization, and serving experiments. The most productive next investment is not simply more epochs; it is cleaner targeted data, uncontaminated evaluation, and a guarded inference system.
