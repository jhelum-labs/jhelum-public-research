1. Executive Summary

Phase 2 established a reproducible baseline for W-1.1 by training a compact decoder-only Transformer on a 500 MB subset of OpenWebText. The run demonstrated stable optimization, strong reduction in training and validation loss, and no clear overfitting. The model learned spelling, punctuation, paragraph structure, and broad English patterns, but its free-running text remains semantically weak and vulnerable to repetition.

Phase 2 verdict Training was technically successful. The resulting checkpoint is a valid research baseline, but it is not yet a reliable assistant or production language model.

3. Phase 2 Architecture

W-1.1 uses a GPT-style decoder stack. It converts token IDs into embeddings, applies repeated causal self-attention and feed-forward transformations, then predicts a probability distribution over the 8,000-token vocabulary.

7. Training Stability Analysis

  • Training loss fell from 9.0489 to 4.4489 in the latest reported log.
  • Validation loss fell from 9.0438 to 4.3268 and continued reaching new lows.
  • Training and validation losses remained close, so there was no clear sign of overfitting.
  • Gradient norms were finite and generally stable; no NaN or infinity failure appeared.
  • The learning rate decayed correctly from its peak toward smaller late-stage updates.
  • A manual KeyboardInterrupt at step 15,500 caused no damage; training resumed correctly at step 15,501 from phase2_latest.pt.

9. Why Perplexity Improved but Generation Remained Weak

Perplexity measures average next-token uncertainty under teacher-forced validation. Generation is harder: every sampled token becomes context for the next prediction, so small mistakes compound. Therefore, a validation perplexity near 75.7 can coexist with poor free-running text.

  • The model has only 17.37M parameters, limiting knowledge and sequence modelling capacity.
  • Only about 40.96M tokens are processed in 20,000 optimizer steps—roughly 34% of the training split.
  • OpenWebText is varied and noisy, which is difficult for a small model.
  • The 256-token context limits long-range information.
  • Generation initially lacked top-p sampling, repetition penalties, n-gram blocking, and clean end-of-text stopping.

Final conclusion Phase 2 achieved its research objective: it proved that the W-1.1 training pipeline can prepare a real dataset, train stably on the GTX 1650, recover from interruption, and learn measurable English patterns. The saved phase2_best.pt checkpoint should be preserved as the OpenWebText baseline for future comparison.

11. Recommended Next Work

Archive phase2_best.pt as the OpenWebText baseline. Improve decoding with top-p sampling, repetition penalties, three-token n-gram blocking, and end-of-text stopping. Then continue pretraining on clean, licensed human-written text while preserving the same tokenizer and architecture. Begin instruction tuning only after short completions are coherent and repetition is controlled.
